    How ifctrano works

    ifctrano reads the geometry of an IFC model, derives the thermal zones and their envelope from it, and writes a trano configuration. trano turns the configuration into a Modelica model for one of several libraries, which OpenModelica simulates. This page explains each step, the assumptions made on the way and what the generated models contain.

    Pipeline

    flowchart LR
    ifc["IFC files<br/>architecture (+ heating)"] --> parse["ifctrano<br/>spaces, boundaries,<br/>constructions, heating"]
    parse --> yaml["trano configuration<br/>(YAML, ifctrano config)"]
    yaml --> trano["trano<br/>network of components"]
    trano --> mo["Modelica model<br/>Buildings, IDEAS,<br/>AixLib reduced order, ISO 13790"]
    mo --> om["OpenModelica<br/>(Docker)"]
    om --> results["Results<br/>.mat file, report"]

    ifctrano create runs the whole chain. ifctrano config stops at the configuration, which can be reviewed and edited before ifctrano from-config builds (and optionally simulates) the model. Both routes produce the same model: the model is always built from the configuration.

    IFC parsing

    What is read

    IFC entity Use
    IfcSpace One thermal zone per space. Required: a model without spaces is rejected.
    IfcWall, IfcSlab, IfcRoof, IfcDoor, IfcWindow Envelope and internal elements of the spaces.
    IfcPlate Internal elements between two spaces only; plates on the envelope are ignored.
    IfcBuildingElementPart Parts of a wall or slab, merged into their parent element.
    IfcMaterialLayerSet (through IfcRelAssociatesMaterial or IfcMaterialLayerSetUsage) Constructions: names and thicknesses of the layers.
    IfcSIUnit (length) Millimetre models are converted to metres.
    IFC4 MEP elements (heat generators, pumps, valves, radiators, ports) Heating system, see Systems.

    IfcRelSpaceBoundary is not used: space boundaries are rarely present, or reliable, in real models. ifctrano derives them from the geometry instead.

    Spaces and boundaries

    For each space, ifctrano:

    1. computes its oriented bounding box, its floor area (footprint of its shape) and its average height (volume over floor area; the bounding box is used when the shape has no footprint);
    2. finds the building elements within 0.5 m of it (clash_clearance_many of IfcOpenShell's geometry tree);
    3. keeps, for each of them, the common surface between the faces of the space's bounding box and of the element's: its area, its outward normal and its polygon;
    4. replaces building element parts by their parent element, and keeps a single slab or roof where several cover the same surface with the same orientation.

    An element whose common surfaces are found with two spaces, facing each other, is an internal element between these spaces: the smaller of the two surfaces is its area. All other boundaries are external.

    Assumptions and known limitations

    • Each IfcSpace is one zone: spaces are not merged into larger zones, and a space with an invalid shape is skipped (and logged).
    • Bounding boxes approximate the geometry: curved or strongly non-convex spaces and elements get approximate areas.
    • Only walls, slabs, roofs, doors and windows bound a space towards the outside; other elements (plates, columns, beams, curtain-wall members as such) are ignored on the envelope.
    • An external boundary exchanges heat with the outdoor air, or with the ground for a floor: unheated neighbouring buildings, crawl spaces or spaces missing from the model are treated as outdoors.
    • Only the thicknesses of the layers come from the IFC model: every material gets the same default thermal properties (conductivity 0.046 W/(m·K), specific heat capacity 940 J/(kg·K), density 80 kg/m³), as IFC models rarely carry them. Edit the material section of the configuration to use real values.

    Wall translation

    Kind of boundary

    Element Tilt Becomes
    Wall, door, window wall external wall (doors too), or window
    Roof ceiling external wall facing up
    Slab, normal pointing up ceiling external wall facing up
    Slab, normal pointing down floor floor on ground
    Element shared by two spaces wall, or floor and ceiling for slabs internal wall between the two zones

    Orientation

    The orientation of a surface is given by its outward normal, measured from the north axis of the model (+y by default, see north_axis). trano and the Modelica libraries use the azimuth of the Buildings library: radians from south, positive towards west.

    Facade faces Azimuth (rad)
    South 0
    West π/2 (1.57)
    North π (3.14)
    East −π/2 (−1.57)

    Vertical surfaces are snapped to the closest of the eight main and intermediate directions. The orientation of roofs and slabs does not change their physics: they are given the azimuth 0.

    Areas

    • Walls are gross facade areas, windows included, as trano expects: trano cuts the windows out of the walls of their orientation.
    • The walls and windows of a space are merged per orientation (one wall and one window area per orientation), with the construction of the first wall.
    • Windows larger than the wall of their orientation (overlapping window boxes on glazed facades) are reduced to 90 % of the wall area. A wall is added where a space has windows without walls.

    Constructions

    • Each IfcMaterialLayerSet becomes a construction named after the layer set (or construction_<id> when unnamed), with one layer per IfcMaterialLayer. Names are made valid Modelica identifiers, and distinct layer sets sharing a name get distinct names.
    • Layers without thickness (membranes, foils) are left out: they add no resistance or capacity to one-dimensional conduction, and trano rejects them. Elements whose layer set has no thick layer get default_construction.
    • Elements without a layer set (no association, IfcMaterialList, constituent sets) get default_construction (0.18 m of the default material); internal elements get default_internal_construction.
    • Windows use a double glazing (3 mm glass, 12.7 mm air, 3 mm glass) with a frame U-value of 1.4 W/(m²·K).

    Modelling approach

    Zones

    Each space is a zone of the chosen library; the envelope, internal walls and windows found above are attached to it.

    Library Zone model Envelope
    Buildings (default) Buildings.ThermalZones.Detailed.MixedAir One-dimensional conduction through each layer, windows with solar transmission, long- and shortwave radiation exchange between surfaces, mixed air volume.
    IDEAS IDEAS.Buildings.Components.Zone Components per wall, window, slab on ground and internal wall.
    reduced_order AixLib ThermalZones.ReducedOrder.ThermalZone Envelope lumped into resistance–capacitance elements per orientation.
    iso_13790 AixLib ThermalZones.ISO13790.Zone5R1C Five resistances and one capacitance (ISO 13790 simple hourly method).

    The detailed libraries model each surface; the reduced-order ones are faster and coarser. The library comparison shows how Buildings and IDEAS differ on the same building. Every library is simulated in CI on the two zones model (tests/test_simulation.py).

    Thermal network and boundary conditions

    • Internal walls couple the zones they separate; both sides keep their thermal mass.
    • External walls and roofs exchange heat with the outdoor air and receive solar radiation according to their orientation and tilt; floors on ground sit on the ground, at 10 °C (283.15 K, trano's default) for Buildings and the reduced-order zone, while IDEAS and ISO 13790 model the ground themselves.
    • Weather: trano's default, the Uccle (Belgium) typical year shipped with IDEAS, for every library.
    • Occupancy and infiltration: trano's default occupancy, occupied from 7:00 to 19:00, with the internal gains gain = [35; 70; 30] (radiant, convective, latent) scaled by heat_gain_if_occupied = 1/6/4, and an air change rate of 0.9 1/h.
    • Initial temperature of the zones: 21 °C.

    All these values are in the configuration written by ifctrano config, where they can be changed.

    Systems

    Without a heating model, the zones are free-floating: no emission or production system is generated. With heating elements in the architecture file or in federated discipline files (--hvac), ifctrano builds the hydronic system described in the heating tutorial:

    IFC trano element Modelica component (Buildings)
    IfcSpaceHeater (radiator) radiator, lumped per space Buildings.Fluid.HeatExchangers.Radiators.RadiatorEN442_2
    Radiator valve valve Buildings.Fluid.Actuators.Valves.TwoWayEqualPercentage
    IfcPump pump Buildings.Fluid.Movers.Preconfigured.SpeedControlled_y (in a trano wrapper)
    IfcValve (mixing) three_way_valve Buildings.Fluid.Actuators.Valves.ThreeWayEqualPercentageLinear
    IfcSensor (temperature) temperature_sensor Buildings.Fluid.Sensors.TemperatureTwoPort
    IfcBoiler boiler, without_storage or default (with a storage tank) variant Buildings.Fluid.Boilers.BoilerPolynomial (and Buildings.Fluid.Storage.StratifiedEnhanced)
    Heat pump (IfcUnitaryEquipment recognised as such, or --heat-generator) boiler, air_water_heat_pump or water_water_heat_pump variant Buildings.Fluid.HeatPumps.ModularReversible.AirToWaterTableData or Buildings.Fluid.HeatPumps.EquationFitReversible
    Radiator not connected to a heat generator radiator, idealbus variant Ideal emitter

    Radiators of a space are lumped into one emitter whose nominal power is the sum of their OutputCapacity. Missing data are filled in and logged: radiators and pumps are sized from the design heat load of their spaces (simplified EN 12831), the heat generator from the radiators it serves. The heat generator example compares a boiler and a heat pump on the same house.

    Control

    trano adds a controller to every element that needs one. The controllers generated for a hydronic system are:

    Controller Controls How
    Emission control (one per heated space) Radiator valve PI control of the zone air temperature (ASHRAE Guideline 36 zone control loop) towards a set point switched by a schedule.
    Collector control (one per circuit) Circulator Pump speed following the most open radiator valve of the circuit; the pump stops when no valve asks for heat (hysteresis).
    Three-way valve control (one per circuit) Mixing valve Proportional control of the supply temperature measured by the circuit's sensor.
    Boiler control Heat generator Fires when the rooms ask for heat; off above an outdoor temperature of 15 °C; supply set point 80 °C.

    The emission control parameters, with trano's defaults:

    Parameter Default Meaning
    temperature_heating_setpoint 294.15 K (21 °C) Set point during occupied hours
    temperature_heating_setback 289 K (15.85 °C) Set point outside occupied hours (night setback)
    schedule 3600*{7, 19} Switching times: occupied from 7:00 to 19:00
    controller_gain 5 Gain of the controller

    They are set under emission_control.parameters of each space in the configuration; the control example compares the default night setback with a constant set point.

    Checking the physics

    tests/physics.py reads the parameters driving the simulation (weather file, zone geometry, envelope areas, orientations and constructions, nominal values of the heating components) from the generated Buildings model. The tests compare them with snapshots, so that a change of ifctrano or trano cannot silently change the physics of the models; every difference with the snapshots of earlier trano versions is listed in tests/test_physics.py with its reason. The examples of the tutorials are simulated in CI for every change.
