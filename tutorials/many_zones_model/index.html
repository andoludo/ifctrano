    Many zones model

    ifctrano makes one thermal zone per IfcSpace, and couples the zones through the walls and slabs they share. This tutorial uses MultiZoneBuilding.ifc from tests/models/space_boundary.

    from pathlib import Path

from ifctrano.building import Building

building = Building.from_ifc(Path("MultiZoneBuilding.ifc"))
building.save_model()

    or, showing the space boundaries and simulating the model:

    ifctrano create MultiZoneBuilding.ifc --show-space-boundaries --simulate-model

    The space boundaries derived from the geometry: external surfaces in red, surfaces shared between spaces in blue.

    Results

    The building has no heating model: the zones are free-floating. With more zones than lines can show clearly, the figure draws the range of the zone temperatures (minimum to maximum) and their mean.

    What to expect. The zones are coupled through the internal walls, so their temperatures follow each other; the zones with more external surface (and less internal gain per volume) are colder.

    What the simulation shows. The thirteen zones stay between 3 and 17 °C, with a mean of about 9 °C over days 3 to 10. The spread between the zones (about 3 K on the mean temperature, from 6.9 °C to 10.0 °C) comes from their exposure: the six identical rooms with 8 m² of external wall and a 4 m² window all average 9.2 to 9.3 °C, while the two zones with the most external wall (45 m² and 29 m²) and no window are the coldest (6.9 °C and 7.3 °C).
